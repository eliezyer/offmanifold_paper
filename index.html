Sleep V1
Geometry Movement Vision Synthesis Paper

Nature Communications, 2026

Movement enters visual cortex. Vision still has to survive.

V1 carries rich signals about ongoing movements while also representing the visual world. This creates a geometric problem: how can movement and sensory activity share the same population without one interfering the other?

This study uses sleep to reveal the intrinsic structure of V1 population activity, then asks how movement and stimulus representations occupy that structure during wakefulness.

One population, multiple variables

V1 jointly represents movement and visual stimuli.

Movement-related activity in V1 is not a small nuisance signal. Running and facial movements can be decoded from population activity, yet the same neurons must still encode visual stimuli. The question is how their coexistence is organized.

An intrinsic reference frame

Sleep reveals the circuit's preferred geometry.

NREM sleep provides a window into population activity with reduced visual input and little overt movement. In that state, V1 expresses structured intrinsic dynamics that are also substantially preserved during wakefulness. Those dynamics give a reference frame for asking where awake movement and stimulus activity live.

Three kinds of subspaces

Not every direction is equally available.

Cross-validated PCA of NREM activity separates state space into three operational subspaces. On-manifold dimensions express more reliable variance than expected by chance. Unstructured dimensions are near chance. Off-manifold dimensions express less variance than chance.

Off-manifold is specifically defined.

These are not arbitrary dimensions outside the intrinsic manifold. They are directions suppressed by intrinsic dynamics during NREM, which makes them a structured part of the circuit's geometry.

Movement follows the dominant modes

Movement lives largely on the manifold.

Facial motion and running speed are preferentially represented in on-manifold dimensions. Because the on-manifold is low-dimensional, higher-dimensional unstructured spaces can contain more total decodable movement signal. But per dimension, movement information is concentrated on-manifold.

Intrinsic dynamics shape the response

The circuit constrains what inputs become.

RNN simulations and optogenetic playback support the idea that intrinsic local dynamics can concentrate diverse inputs into overlapping subspaces. But those dynamics do not determine the detailed representation. The fine structure still depends on the specific input pattern.

Visual information splits

Vision uses both intrinsic and suppressed directions.

Natural-scene identity is encoded in both on-manifold and off-manifold dimensions. Per dimension, stimulus information is strongest on-manifold. But because the off-manifold subspace has more dimensions, it carries a large share of total stimulus information.

Where signals meet

On-manifold is the shared subspace.

Movement and stimulus representations interact in the on-manifold subspace. The key evidence is asymmetric: stimulus identity was not decoded from the movement subspace, but facial motion could be predicted from the stimulus-encoding subspace. This interaction was additive and localized to on-manifold dimensions.

A quieter coding space

Off-manifold is spared from movement.

Off-manifold dimensions are suppressed during intrinsic NREM dynamics and contain relatively little movement-related activity. Visual information encoded there therefore experiences less movement interference.

Cellular structure

Choristers can become sparse.

On- and off-manifold activity both involve chorister neurons, whose spontaneous activity is strongly coupled to the population. The difference is pattern structure: on-manifold activity is population-dense, while off-manifold stimulus coding reflects choristers participating in sparse stimulus-evoked patterns.

A second function for sparseness

Sparse responses access the off-manifold.

Sparse coding is often discussed in terms of efficiency. Here it also has a geometric role. When visual stimuli evoke sparse patterns in chorister neurons, activity can access off-manifold dimensions that movement rarely enters, improving separability between movement and stimulus representations.

Integration and segregation

One geometry supports two computations.

Low-dimensional intrinsic dynamics concentrate movement and part of the visual representation into a shared on-manifold space, where they can interact. The same dynamics define suppressed off-manifold directions that sparse stimulus responses can use with less movement interference.

Together, intrinsic dynamics and sparse coding help V1 integrate movement with vision while partially segregating visual information from movement.

What remains open

The geometry is clear. The mechanism is not finished.

The study identifies a population-level organization, not a complete circuit mechanism. The models show proof of principle, and the optogenetic experiments support intrinsic constraints, but the biological sources of off-manifold stimulus responses remain open. Head fixation also limits the range of natural movements.
